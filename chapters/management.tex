\section{Cost estimation}

The ECSS-M-ST-60C standard \cite{ECSS-M-ST-60C} indicates that costs and schedule management are defined as a ``collective system of processes and actions in support of project management''. Specifically, the definition of cost encompasses resources beyond the financial, such as human resources, infrastructure, and consumables.

This section covers the Cost Breakdown Structure (\ac{CBS}) for the Catarina-A1. It presents a graphical breakdown of all project costs separated into cost categories in order to facilitate the tracking of each category, and the Cost Estimate Report (\ac{CER}), which is the programmed estimate of such costs throughout the project.

\subsection{Cost Breakdown Structure}

The \ac{CBS} illustrated in \autoref{fig:cbs}, presented in the ECSS-M-ST-60C standard of July 31, 2008 \cite{ECSS-M-ST-60C}, defines five main cost categories for space projects, which can be broken down into subcategories of costs. The categories are: Direct human resources, Internal infrastructure, Supplies and other direct costs, Sub-contracts, and Expenses not linked to production. 

Quantities such as margin and profit are not included in the CBS as they are not considered to be direct costs of interest to the system supplier at this project stage.

\end{figure}

In \autoref{fig:cbs}, the boxes highlighted in blue indicate that such categories may undergo subsequent break-ups and the yellow boxes indicate categories and subcategories that potentially include indirect costs.

The CBS defined for space system Catarina-A1 of Catarina Constellation is illustrated in \autoref{fig:cbs-a1}. In this CBS, references can be made to the organizational chart presented in the delta-Mission Design Review (D-MDR), and to the product tree (\autoref{sec:product-tree}).

The Human Resources category includes the official collaborators: four professors and twelve students from the Federal University of Santa Catarina (\ac{UFSC}) and the team provided by the National Institute for Space Research in Natal (\ac{INPE-COENE}) for the mission, yet to be defined.

Regarding the internal facilities, references are made to: (\textit{i}) the UFSC Spacelab laboratory that will be responsible for testing, manufacturing and integration of the Catarina-A1; (\textit{ii}) the laboratories of the Thermal Fluid Flow Group (\ac{T2F}) that will be used to perform vibration and vacuum tests; (\textit{iii}) to the ISI Embedded Systems laboratory that will be used for testing embedded systems; and (\textit{iv}) to the infrastructure of the Estação Multi-Missão de Natal at INPE-RN that will assist in mission control.

In the category of supplies, the Product Tree itself stands out as a guide for costs with materials and parts (\autoref{fig:product-tree}), in addition to the testing and external purchases. Finally, as costs are not linked to production, general and administrative expenses stand out.

\subsubsection{Cost Sensitivity Analysis} \label{sec:cost_sensitivity_analysis}

Referring to \autoref{sec:cbs}, specifically \autoref{CBS_A1}, stand out the cost categories of:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{External Services:} the Launch is included in this category, and it has an extremely variable cost, depending on the launch location, the time of year, the number of items launched in the chosen launcher, and the economic and political conditions - both international and internal at the country where the launch will take place;
    \item \textbf{Materials and Parts:} the variation of the dollar and the lack of components in the market generate insecurity regarding some specific components of the Product Tree and their respective parts;
    \item \textbf{Transport and Insurance:} highly dependent on launch parameters, this category also suffers from the same restrictions as the first item.
\end{itemize}

Such categories generate risks linked to project cost and should be carefully evaluated and revisited whenever possible in future revisions of this document. Also, as seen in \autoref{sec:cost_risk_assessment}, the risk assessments related to the cost category are listed. It also suffers from the same restrictions as the first item.

\subsubsection{Cost risk assessment} \label{sec:cost_risk_assessment}
The project risk management documents \cite{RMP} and  \cite{Risk_Report} list risks linked to costs (or collaterally to purchases) up to the present stage:

\begin{itemize}
    \item RSK-1: Unable to obtain additional financial resources to complete the mission;
    \item RSK-5: Significant rise in the dollar may cause the project to not have enough resources to acquire the systems;
    \item RSK-17: Materials resources not sufficient for preliminary tests.
\end{itemize}

It is intended to evaluate more rigorously, until the following review, the risks involved in the launch (and consequently the cost categories that are affected by it) to develop the assessment and mitigation steps best.

For more in-depth information on the risks already listed, see UFSC-CAT-A1-MNG-RMP-0001\_A \cite{RMP}.

\subsubsection{Quality of Estimate} \label{sec:quality_estimate}

Since the current project was conceived and is being developed from the flight legacy of the nanosatellite FloripaSat-1 \cite{FloripaSat-1}, the costs presented in the \autoref{sec:cost_breakdown_tables} refer to work packages (\acs{WP}) with a high level of maturity \cite{WBS_A1}.

Applying the classification methodology of standard No 17R-97 of the International Association for the Advancement of Cost Engineering (\ac{AACE}) \cite{Norma_AACE}, which can be seen in the table illustrated by \autoref{AACEI_Figura1}, it is possible to define a classification of such cost estimate from the percentage of maturity of the WPs.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/quadro_NormaAACEI_EN.pdf}
    \caption{Generic classification matrix for costs estimative \cite{Norma_AACE}.}
    \label{AACEI_Figura1}
\end{figure}

It is estimated that at the end of the \ac{SRR} the WP3 - Engineering Model and Flight Model preliminary definition \cite{WBS_A1} will be complete, describing a maturity level of the 10 to 40 \%.

The secondary characteristics of the cost estimative are given by:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Final usage:} proportional to the maturity level of the project deliverables, this characteristic identifies the specific purpose of the estimate. Within the framework of the SRR, the project will be in the Control or bidding/proposal phase;
    \item \textbf{Methodology:} identifies the source of the cost estimates, ie, the nature of the variables used to create the CBTs. As these are definitive measurements, the methodology is said to be deterministic;
    \item \textbf{Expected accuracy range:} depending on market variations and the pre-established budget, it is estimated that the accuracy range is, within the SRR framework, +1/+3 \%;
    \item \textbf{Effort for elaboration:} defines the percentage concerning the total costs of the project consumed in the analyzed phase. For the SRR framework, it is estimated that 5 to 20 \% of the total cost has been consumed.
\end{itemize}

The main characteristic of the matrix is a previous indication that CBTs fit into Class 3 of the cost estimate; however, the secondary characteristics tend to increase this classification. Taking into account the predefined budget and the flight inheritance used, it is possible to classify the cost estimate as \textbf{Class 2}.

\subsubsection{Recomendations} \label{sec:Recomendations}

This document compiles the cost estimation process for the Catarina-A1 of the Catarina Constellation - Fleet A project, taking into account the time of the SRR report. CBS, estimation quality, sensitivity analysis, and primary cost risk assessments are defined and must be updated for each deliverable to ensure that the previously established budget is met.

For future versions of this document, it is recommended that the costs listed in \autoref{sec:cost_sensitivity_analysis} be monitored and updated more frequently. Also, keep the methodology used in \autoref{sec:quality_estimate} updated to ensure that the evolution from Class 2 to Class 1 occurs as efficiently and quickly as possible.

Specifically for the items described in \autoref{sec:cost_sensitivity_analysis}, it is recommended to increase budgets for the launch service and try to find alternative partnerships for it, always meeting the technical requirements to guarantee the system's useful life. As for the lack of supplies, it is necessary to insist on searching for alternative suppliers or technologies developed in-house.

\section{Risk Management}

A risk is an event that threatens the project's success, even if partially. Therefore, this plan aims to help identifying adverse events at an early stage, handle them, and mitigate.

The development of this project will be based on a qualitative risk analysis standard. This depends on crossing two metrics: the probability of risk occurrence and the impact of risk occurrence. \autoref{likelihood} shows definitions of the probability of occurrence categorization.

Five levels of impact define the occurrence impact metric:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{5 (Catastrophic):} In general, these are risks that, if materialized, make the Subject or Activity unfeasible or end. Some examples of the description by feature:
        \begin{itemize}
           \item  \textit{Financial Resources:} Significant increase in expenses that makes the continuation of the project or activity unfeasible or causes its termination;
           \item  \textit{Material Resources:} loss of materials that makes the continuation of the project or activity unfeasible or causes its termination;
           \item  \textit{Temporal Resources:} delay that makes the continuation of the project or activity unfeasible or causes its termination;
           \item  \textit{Human Resources:} Death of one or more people. Evasion of more than 95\% of those involved; and 
           \item  \textit{Organizational Image Resources:} Loss of total institutional credibility.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{4 (Critical):} Overall, these are risks that, if materialized, make it very difficult to complete or progress the Subject/Activity and may raise considerations of finishing the Subject or Activity. Some examples of the description by resource:
    \begin{itemize}
       \item \textit{ Financial Resources:} Increase of over 50\% in expenses;
       \item  \textit{Material Resources:} Material loss that cannot be replaced in a timely manner and that causes a significant loss of permanent performance of the system;
       \item  \textit{Temporal Resources:} delay of more than 80\% in the estimated time in the conclusion of the Subject/Activity;
       \item  \textit{Human Resources: }Permanent disability of one or more people. Evasion between 70\% and 95\% of those involved; and
       \item  \textit{Organizational Image Resources:} Very strong loss of institutional credibility.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{3 (Major):} Overall, these are risks that, if materialized, make it difficult to complete or progress the Subject/Activity but do not usually raise considerations about ending the Subject or Activity. Some examples of the description by resource:
    \begin{itemize}
       \item  \textit{Financial Resources:} Increase in subject/activity expenses between 15\% and 50\%;
       \item  \textit{Material Resources:} Material loss capable of being replaced in a timely manner, but causing significant temporary system loss;
       \item  \textit{Temporal Resources: }delay between 40\% and 80\% in the estimated time in the conclusion of the Subject/Activity;
       \item  \textit{Human Resources: }Temporary disability of one or more people. Evasion between 40\% and 70\% of those involved; and
       \item  \textit{Organizational Image Resources:} Loss of significant institutional credibility.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2 (Minor):} Overall, these are risks that, if materialized, significantly hamper the completion or progress of the Subject/Activity, but definitely do not raise the consideration of termination. Some examples of the description by feature:
    \begin{itemize}
       \item  \textit{Financial Resources: }Increase in subject/activity expenses between 5\% and 15\%;
       \item  \textit{Material Resources: }Material loss capable of being replaced in a timely manner and causing a temporary loss of system performance;
       \item  \textit{Temporal Resources:} delay between 10\% and 20\% in the estimated time for completion of the Subject/Activity;
       \item \textit{ Human Resources:} Minor injuries to one or more people. Evasion between 25\% and 40\% of those involved; and
       \item  \textit{Organizational Image Resources:} Some loss of institutional credibility.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{1 (Insignificant):} Overall, these are risks that, if materialized, make it a little difficult to complete or progress the Subject/Activity. Some examples of the description by resource:
    \begin{itemize}
         \item  \textit{Financial Resources:} Increase in subject/activity expenses by up to 5\%;
         \item  \textit{Material Resources:} Loss of material that does not cause temporary loss of performance;
         \item  \textit{Temporal Resources:} delay of up to 10\% in the estimated time in the conclusion of the Subject/Activity;
         \item  \textit{Human Resources:} Minor injuries to one or more people. Evasion of less than 25\% of those involved; and
         \item  \textit{Organizational Image Resources:} Little loss of institutional credibility.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
